ABOUT ME

I am a PhD candidate at Penn's Artificial Intelligence in Biomedical Imaging Lab, advised by Christos Davatzikos and Hao Huang, with research mentorship from Pratik Chaudhari.

My research develops generative and representation-learning methods that distinguish normal biological variability from disease-specific effects. I am particularly interested in personalized normative modeling, disease subtyping, counterfactual generation, and multimodal biomarkers of aging and neurodegeneration.

Before my PhD, I worked at Biogen, Harvard Medical School, and Regeneron Pharmaceuticals on medical-imaging pipelines, computational research integrity, and disease quantification.

Research

Models for biological variability and disease heterogeneity

I build methods that move beyond population-average case-control effects toward subject-specific reference states, deviations, subtypes, and trajectories.

Structured representation learning

CASL-VAE

How can we discover disease subtypes and severity when healthy and affected samples are unpaired?

CASL-VAE separates shared biological variability from target-specific variation, then decomposes the salient space into discrete subtypes and continuous within-subtype heterogeneity. The same formulation supports paired counterfactual generation.

NeurIPS 2026Unpaired dataSemi-supervised clusteringCounterfactuals
Personalized normative modeling

RESTORE

What would a patient's measurements look like without the disease-related effect?

RESTORE uses simulation-based self-supervision to estimate a personalized healthy reference and subject-specific deviation map. The framework is evaluated across structural MRI, longitudinal aging, and CSF proteomics.
